# Information Organization Skills for Better Decisions

Decision Literacy uses options, tradeoffs, reasons, probabilities, outcomes, and reflection. Information Organization helps kids sort decision information so they can compare choices without everything blending together.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Information Organization Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/information-organization), connected to the clear thinking this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Decisions get easier when options and tradeoffs are visible.** A table beats a jumble.
- **Reasons, feelings, risks, and evidence are different types of information.** Sorting them keeps the comparison honest.
- **Decision journals need labels** so future-you can review them.
- **Outcomes and process notes should stay connected.** The result only teaches you something next to the reasoning.

## When this shows up

- When comparing options
- When sorting pros, cons, risks, and unknowns
- When tracking a decision journal
- When reviewing luck vs. skill
- When evidence and feelings get mixed together
- When a decision has too much information

## Tools that help

- **Option / tradeoff table** — one row per option.
- **Reasons vs. feelings vs. evidence sort** — three piles before deciding.
- **Decision journal label** — topic + choice + date.
- **Outcome + process note** — keep them on the same page.
- **Example / non-example** — for decision terms like risk, bias, or expected value.

:::tip Information Organization Moment
A decision gets easier when the information has places to go. Try sorting notes into options, reasons, risks, and unknowns. The choice may still be hard, but the pile will be easier to think with.
:::

:::caution Everyday organization skills, not a replacement for support
These are everyday information organization tools, not therapy, disability evaluation, academic intervention, legal advice, medical advice, or a replacement for trusted adults, educators, caregivers, accommodations, or qualified support. Kids should not be treated as lazy or broken when information feels messy or hard to organize. If information feels consistently overwhelming, inaccessible, unsafe, private, sensitive, or too big, involve a trusted adult and appropriate support.
:::
